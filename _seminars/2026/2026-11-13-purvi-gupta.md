Runge’s theorem states that a function holomorphic near a compact subset of the complex plane can be uniformly approximated by rational functions with poles off the
set; polynomials suffice when its complement is connected. In higher dimensions, analogous approximation theorems involve geometric conditions known as rational and
polynomial convexity. These conditions, and the geometric questions they raise, are the focus of this talk. In the first half, we will introduce these notions for
compact subsets of $\mathbb C^n$ and explore several techniques for establishing them, working throughout with concrete examples. No prior knowledge of several complex
variables will be assumed. 

In the second half, we ask how many complex dimensions are needed to embed a closed real $n$-manifold as a polynomially convex set. It turns out that $\mathbb C^n$ is
too small: no embedding of a real $n$-manifold into $\mathbb C^n$ can have polynomially convex image. Remarkably, one additional complex dimension always suffices for
a topological embedding, but whether $\mathbb C^{n+1}$ also suffices for a smooth polynomially convex embedding remains open. We will explore how far current
techniques take us towards the expected embedding dimension.
